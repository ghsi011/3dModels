# Assembly & field instructions — Nuc bottle feeder

## Which through-roof unit? Measure your headspace first

Two interchangeable through-roof units share the same nut, gaskets, plug and adapter:

- **`drip_barrel`** — for **6–10 mm** frame-to-ceiling headspace (the current hive,
  measured). Pail-feeder principle: an integral orifice plate (4 × Ø1.2 mm) sits at the
  ceiling plane; vacuum + surface tension hold the syrup and the bees drink directly at
  the holes. Only 5 mm protrudes below the ceiling (4 bearing spokes + a Ø20 feeding
  mouth); bees walk in through 4 open windows between the spokes. Bee-tight even with
  the bottle off (Ø1.2 holes). Zero moving parts, zero joints below the roof (§1.3).
- **`body`** (pool tray) — needs **≥35 mm** headspace (tray 17.4 mm + bee gap). Keep for
  taller hives.

Install, clamp, bottle swap and adapter use are identical for both.

## Parts

body (tray+barrel) **or drip_barrel** · clamp nut · 2× TPU gasket washer · transport
plug · **bottle adapter (cap-connector)** · 1 L PCO-1881 still-water bottle (the feeder
fits any bottle with this neck — nearly every Israeli 0.5/1/1.5 L still bottle).

The **bottle adapter** is a two-ended coupler, fully independent of the roof clamp:
- **top**: a female PCO-1881 cap the bottle screws into (own TPU gasket inside),
- **bottom**: the same male stub as the transport plug — screws into the feeder socket,
- **between them**: an orifice plate (7 × Ø1.5 mm holes). No moving parts: flip the
  bottle+adapter and after a ~2 ml dribble the vacuum self-locks the syrup (pail-feeder
  physics) until it's screwed into the feeder.

## Hive prep (bench, roof panel off the hive)

1. Pick the spot: the tray is Ø150 — keep its center ≥ 90 mm from hive walls so the rim
   gap stays open, roughly over the frame seams you want to feed.
2. **Drill Ø44 mm** (hole saw) straight through roof + ceiling as one hole. Range Ø42–46
   works; the clamp faces (Ø60 below, Ø80 above) cover any of these. Deburr.
3. Drop the **gasket** into the socket (flat washer onto the internal shoulder — poke it
   flat with a finger).

## Install (no tools)

4. From below, push the barrel up through the hole (tray in hand).
5. From above, spin the **nut** down the barrel (2-start thread ≈ 3 turns) and hand-
   tighten firmly while holding the tray. The skirt should end up ~3 mm above the roof —
   it must NOT touch (if it does, your roof is thicker than 30 mm).
6. Screw in the **plug** until the hive is populated with a bottle. The hive is now
   bee-tight, rain-shedding, and structurally done.
7. **Anti-ant:** smear a finger of Vaseline/barrier grease around the barrel *under the
   skirt*. Renew monthly.
8. Works clamped anywhere in **8–30 mm** roof thickness; re-snug the nut at your first
   inspection (plastic seats into wood) and at routine visits.

## Filling / bottle swap — with the adapter (recommended)

1. Fill the bottle with 1:1 syrup **to the brim** (less headspace = less thermal
   push-out) — bottle stays upright the whole time.
2. Screw the **adapter** onto the upright bottle, hand-tight (its internal gasket seals
   against the bottle lip). Carry to the hive.
3. Flip bottle+adapter. Expect a 1–2 ml dribble from the orifice holes, then it stops by
   itself — the vacuum has locked it. No rush from here.
4. Unscrew the plug (or the spent bottle+adapter) from the feeder socket; the barrel
   column (~15 ml) drains into the tray.
5. Screw the adapter's male stub into the socket to a firm hand stop (main gasket
   compressing). Flow resumes through the orifice plate; normal glug regulation follows.
6. Watch 30 s: a few glugs, bottle flanks panel in, then quiet. Quiet = sealed.

## Filling / bottle swap — bare bottle (no adapter)

1–2. As above but leave the bottle capped with its own cap for the walk.
3. Unscrew plug/spent bottle. Uncap the fresh bottle, invert it over the socket, and
   screw it in — **commit: one smooth motion, ~2 turns.** It glugs ~30 ml while the
   threads engage; the tray is sized to swallow that. Tighten to a firm hand stop.
4. Watch 30 s as above. Continuous trickle = not sealed — reseat the gasket/bottle.

Level checks are by eye from outside — the bottle is the gauge. Swap when empty; bees
empty the tray remainder themselves.

## Removal / season end

Unscrew bottle → screw in plug (hive stays bee-tight) → or remove entirely: spin nut off,
drop body out, push a wine cork / Ø45 disc into the hole. Wash all parts in warm water
(≤50 °C — no dishwasher); everything is open-bore and brushable. Store out of the sun.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Tray floods to the rim | leak: gasket not seated / bottle not tight / cracked print | remove bottle, reseat gasket, retighten; bench 24 h leak test the body |
| Tray bone-dry, bottle full | outlet airlocked after fill | press the bottle flank once (forces a glug) |
| Pool creeps up hot afternoons then recedes | normal thermal breathing | none needed; shade/foil the bottle if it approaches the boss tops |
| Bees not using it | access | verify under-tray gap 6–9 mm (measure headspace!), dribble syrup on the rim once |
| Feeder rotates when swapping bottles | nut not snug | re-tighten nut |
| Adapter keeps dripping after flip | air leak at bottle↔adapter joint | re-tighten bottle into adapter; check the adapter's internal TPU gasket is seated flat |
| No flow with adapter installed | orifice holes gummed with crystallized syrup | unscrew, rinse warm water, brush the plate |
